Two things that were actually hard

Shipping fast is easy to claim. These two are the ones I would want to be judged on, because both were live systems with real money moving through them and neither was solved by guessing.

Performance · Accounting

272 queries down to 7

Symptom

The chart of accounts was the slowest screen in the accounting product. It felt broken rather than slow, and it was the screen finance users opened first.

Cause

A classic N+1 that had grown teeth. Every account pulled its own related rows, and the tree structure multiplied it. One page load meant 272 round trips to PostgreSQL.

Constraint

The account type column is a native Postgres enum, which rules out the bulk-create shortcut normally reached for here. The fix had to come from the read path itself.

Result 272 → 7

A 97% cut in queries. The slowest screen in the product became an instant one.

Reliability · Financial core

The pool that was never reused

Symptom

Intermittent 500s in the live financial core. Not reproducible on demand, not tied to any one endpoint, and hitting the payments path.

Cause

Connection-pool exhaustion. Each request was taking two connections against a pool of fifteen, so a modest burst of concurrent traffic emptied it.

The counterintuitive part

Persistent connections are supposed to help. Under ASGI they were making it worse: every request lands on a fresh thread, so a connection is opened and never reused. I measured it rather than assuming, and reuse was exactly zero.

Result Payments restored

An entire class of outage removed, and a setting that reads as an optimization everywhere else correctly identified as a leak in this one.
